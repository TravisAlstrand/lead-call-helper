Why this works: It confirms the scope of the request. If the lead form already gives us this information, don't make the prospect repeat it—move forward.

Current Insurance

Can you please confirm your current age, OR for more accurate quotes your date of birth?

Can you please confirm the current age of all family members who would need coverage, including yourself, and let me know if they're male or female?

Why this works: For an individual, date of birth can improve quote accuracy. For a family, asking for ages and applicable rating details in one grouped question is efficient and avoids making the process feel tedious. Collect only the details required for the specific quote.

Why this works: Asking about both conditions and medications can prompt a more complete answer. A prospect may not think of a diagnosis as a 'pre-existing condition,' but may mention the medication they take for it. Follow up respectfully and record the prospect's own words.

Health Notes

Are there any upcoming procedures, recent hospitalizations, or ongoing treatments we should account for when we look at eligibility and coverage?

So are you primarily focused on catastrophic type coverage so those unexpected major events don't turn into a financial crisis or are you more so needing routine, day-to-day doctor coverage?

Why this works: This lets the prospect explain what matters most in their own words. A catastrophic-event priority may point us toward evaluating certain limited-benefit or indemnity-style options; frequent routine-care needs may make comprehensive major medical coverage more relevant. 'Both' is common, so do not get tripped up with a 'Both' response just acknowledge their reply and move on to the next question.

Coverage Type

Are you primarily focused on just health coverage, or would you also like to see the cost and value for dental or vision coverage? I can make those all separate line items if you like.

Why this works: It gives the prospect control over what gets quoted while allowing the agent to identify additional needs. Either answer is fine; respect the preference and record it.

Self-Employed Income

I don't need to know them, but do you have an EIN for that business, or is it under your Social Security number?

A sole proprietorship is the type of business, not a tax ID. A sole prop can have an EIN or can operate under a Social Security number. Record which ID they actually have.

And lastly, so I know that I'm bringing the right options to the table, would you mind sharing what your estimated gross household income is?

A ballpark figure is fine, it doesn't need to be down to the penny or anything.

Why this works: You lead the process by offering a clear next step and two real appointment choices. The prospect retains agency by choosing the time. Don't say 'I have' a time unless it is actually available, and put the appointment on the calendar while you have them.
